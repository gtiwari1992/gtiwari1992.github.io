About

From genomes to clinical outcomes

I am a Senior Data Analyst in Cardiovascular Research at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, where I lead computational analyses of genomic, proteomic and metabolomic data from epidemiological and clinical cohorts. My work spans genome-wide and phenome-wide association studies, polygenic risk scores optimised for diverse populations, and integrative multi-omic models that link molecular measurements to longitudinal clinical outcomes.

Findings from this work have appeared in Nature Metabolism, Circulation, Diabetes Care, JACC: Basic to Translational Science and other flagship journals of the American Heart Association. I collaborate closely with cardiologists, epidemiologists and translational scientists, mentor doctoral and postdoctoral researchers on methodology, and contribute to NIH R01 and U01 grant applications and consortium-level research strategy.

Before moving into biomedical research I worked in financial data science at ICICI Bank and in software engineering at Accenture. I hold graduate degrees in data analytics and finance alongside an engineering undergraduate degree, which is why I tend to think about research problems as both statistical questions and production systems.

Research

Four threads that run through my work

Hover a card to explore it, or jump to the related publications with one click.

Multi-omic integration

Bridging plasma proteomics and metabolomics with longitudinal clinical phenotypes to find biomarkers of diabetes, cardiomyopathy and coronary disease, then mapping them onto pathways with network-based systems biology.

  • Proteomics
  • Metabolomics
  • Pathway enrichment
  • MESA

Statistical genetics & polygenic risk

Genome-wide and phenome-wide association studies, and polygenic risk score methods optimised for diverse ancestries, using biobank-scale resources such as UK Biobank and All of Us with cross-cohort replication.

  • GWAS
  • PheWAS
  • Polygenic risk scores
  • UK Biobank
  • All of Us

Machine learning for risk prediction

Supervised and unsupervised models for incident-event prediction and diagnostic biomarker discovery, wrapped in automated feature engineering and validation pipelines so results hold up outside the training cohort.

  • Risk prediction
  • Biomarker discovery
  • Model validation
  • Feature engineering

Exercise & cardiometabolic health

Molecular responses to acute and habitual exercise in the NIH MoTrPAC consortium and All of Us, including circulating metabolites that mediate cardiorespiratory fitness and the timing of exercise in relation to cardiometabolic disease.

  • MoTrPAC
  • Exercise physiology
  • Cardiorespiratory fitness
  • All of Us
